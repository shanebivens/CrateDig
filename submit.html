One track. Something almost nobody has heard, or nobody has played in years. It does not have to be great. It does not have to be something you like.

A link on its own is enough. Paste one and the artist and track fill themselves in, from YouTube, Spotify or SoundCloud. Everything after that is optional, including which kind of find it is: how many people have played it usually settles that, and when it does not, somebody decides when the track gets written up.

What happens next

  1. The form hands you a GitHub issue with every field already filled in.
  2. Anything you left blank gets read from the link again on the way in.
  3. You press Create. That is the only step on your side.
  4. A bot files it and replies within a minute.
  5. Every drop holds two spots for what people bring back, newest first.
  6. Somebody else falls into it. Then it is their turn to bring one back.

One track at a time, on purpose. Playlists are not taken, because a pick should be something you chose and have something to say about, not something a machine pulled out of your listening.

Submitting needs a free GitHub account, which is the one piece of friction left. Nothing here ever asks for a password to a music service, a cookie, or access to your listening.

CrateDig links to music. It does not host it or copy it. Every recording belongs to whoever made it.
